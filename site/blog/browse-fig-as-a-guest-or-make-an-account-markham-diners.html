Browsing FIG as a guest: what you can do before you make an account

You can look around FIG without signing up. What a guest can see, what needs an account, how to use the map to choose a place, and a short routine for deciding where to eat.

The short answer

Yes, you can use FIG without an account. Open www.figpromo.ca/app and Home works as a guest: you can browse places, menus, reviews and live deals on a map. You only need an account when you want to claim a deal, order, save a place or write a review. FIG is free for diners.

What a guest can do

Think of guest mode as window shopping. You can walk down the street, read the menus in the windows and see which places have a deal on right now, before you commit to anything. That is useful on its own, because choosing where to eat is often the longest part of going out.

Why browsing first is a good habit

Most bad meals out come from deciding in a hurry. You are hungry, you are standing on a plaza and you pick the first place with a short line. Looking first, even for two minutes, changes that. You find out what is open, what a main costs and what other people thought of it.

Because location is optional and never limits a deal, you can also look ahead. If you work in one part of Markham and live in another, you can look at both and decide which suits tonight.

A five-step routine for choosing a place

  1. Pick the kind of meal. A quick lunch, a family dinner and a snack with a friend are different jobs. Decide first, so you are not comparing a coffee shop with a sit-down restaurant.
  2. Scan the map. Look for places near where you will be, and notice which ones have a live deal right now.
  3. Open the menu. Check that there is something you want at a price you are happy with. A deal on a dish you do not like is not a deal.
  4. Read the reviews. Reviews come only from scanned visits, so each one is from someone who actually went. How to read a restaurant's reviews on FIG shows what to look for.
  5. Decide how you will pay. For a dine-in deal, you show the code and pay the restaurant as usual. For takeout, you pay in the app at checkout.

What the map shows, and what it does not

The map is a good way to see what is nearby and what is on offer now. It does not tell you how busy a place is, whether a table is available or whether the kitchen has run out of a dish. For a larger group, or if you have dietary needs, a quick call to the restaurant is still the best step. Eating out with dietary needs or allergies covers what to ask.

When it is worth making an account

Make an account when you find a deal you want to use. Claiming is one tap, and then you show a QR code that staff scan at the restaurant. A few things are worth knowing before you do:

  • A deal code works once.
  • If the restaurant lets a deal be used more than once (up to 20 times, with an optional wait between uses), your screen shows a new code after each scan.
  • You can claim a deal once a day.
  • Restaurants never see your phone number.

The value of a deal is the saving on the meal itself.

A worked example: two minutes of looking

Choosing lunch (example)
  • You have about $15 to spend on lunch, all in, and you can walk ten minutes.
  • On the map you see six places nearby, and two of them have a deal on right now.
  • You open the menus. One deal is on a dish you like. The other is on something you would not order.
  • You read three reviews for the first place, and they match what you want: a quick lunch.

Total time: about two minutes. You walk over knowing what you will order, and you claim the deal on the way.

The numbers are only an example. The point is the order of steps: look, read, then claim.

If you are not sure about a deal

Read the terms on the deal before you go. If anything is unclear, such as the hours it is live or whether it applies to your dish, ask the staff when you arrive. Everyone does better when the deal is understood on both sides of the counter. If a code will not scan, what to check and what to say has a short checklist.

Using FIG with friends or family

Browsing as a guest is also a good way to settle a group decision. Open the map together, look at two or three places and read the menus out loud. Everyone can see the same thing without anyone needing to sign up first. Once the group has chosen, one person makes an account and claims the deal, or each person claims their own, since a diner can claim a deal once a day. If the group is large, call ahead as well. Group dining deals has more on choosing a place and splitting the bill.

Privacy, in plain words

Looking around as a guest asks nothing of you. When you do make an account, you are in control of what you do with it: you claim, order, save and review by choice. Restaurants never see diners' phone numbers, and location is optional. If you have a question about your account or about FIG, you can reach FIG on the contact page at www.figpromo.ca/contact.
