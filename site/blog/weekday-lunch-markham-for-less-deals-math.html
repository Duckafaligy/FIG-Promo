Weekday lunch in Markham for less: a work-week guide, with the math

Five lunches out is a real number once HST and tip are in. A way to look at a work week of lunches, where a deal helps, and the arithmetic for a dollar-off deal and a 2 for 1 with a colleague.

The short answer

Count a weekday lunch at its full price: the menu price, HST and a tip. In the example below, a $12 lunch costs $15.36, so five a week is $76.80. A $3 off deal on one of them saves about $3.39, and a 2 for 1 with a colleague saves about $6.78 each. Deals help most on the lunches you were already going to buy.

Start with the real price

The number on the menu is not what leaves your account. In Ontario, HST is 13% on most restaurant meals, and a tip goes on top. This post uses 15% for the tip only to make the arithmetic concrete. Use whatever you normally leave.

One $12 lunch, all in (example)
  • Menu price: $12.00.
  • HST at 13%: $12.00 × 0.13 = $1.56.
  • Tip at 15% of the menu price: $12.00 × 0.15 = $1.80.
  • Total: $12.00 + $1.56 + $1.80 = $15.36.

Five of these in a work week: 5 × $15.36 = $76.80. Four weeks: $307.20.

If you are used to thinking of lunch as "twelve bucks", that is a surprise. It is also a reason to look for a saving, even a small one.

Where a deal fits in a work week

A deal is worth the most on a lunch you would have bought anyway. A saving on a meal you did not need is not a saving. Here is a simple way to sort a week:

DayType of lunchDeal to look for
MondayPacked lunch from homeNone needed
TuesdayOut, on your ownDollar off or percent off
WednesdayOut with a colleague2 for 1
ThursdayPacked lunch or leftoversNone needed
FridayOut, a treatWhichever is live near you

This is only an example of a week. Yours will look different, and that is fine. The point is to choose the days, rather than let the week choose them for you.

A dollar-off deal on your own lunch

A $3 off deal takes $3.00 off the menu price, and the HST is worked out on the lower price. That means the saving is a little more than $3.00.

The same lunch with $3 off (example)
  • Price after the deal: $12.00 − $3.00 = $9.00.
  • HST at 13%: $9.00 × 0.13 = $1.17.
  • Tip, still on the $12.00 menu price: $1.80.
  • Total: $9.00 + $1.17 + $1.80 = $11.97.
  • Saving: $15.36 − $11.97 = $3.39.

The extra $0.39 is the HST on the $3.00. If you tip on the lower price instead, you save a bit more, because the tip is smaller too. Either way, the saving is real but modest, so it is best on days when you are going out regardless.

The tax rule behind this is that a store's own coupon or discount lowers the price before tax is worked out. The Canada Revenue Agency sets this out in its guide for GST/HST registrants (RC4022, "Coupons").

A 2 for 1 with a colleague

A 2 for 1 is the bigger saving, and it needs two people. Two colleagues each order a $12 lunch, and the second lunch costs nothing.

Two $12 lunches on a 2 for 1 (example)
  • Price after the deal: $12.00 for the two of you.
  • HST at 13%: $12.00 × 0.13 = $1.56.
  • Tip, on the $24.00 menu value: $24.00 × 0.15 = $3.60.
  • Total: $12.00 + $1.56 + $3.60 = $17.16, or $8.58 each.
  • Without the deal: $15.36 each. Saving: $15.36 − $8.58 = $6.78 each.

If you split the bill, group dining deals shows how to do it fairly, including how to handle a tip.

What about tipping and the staff?

It is fair to tip on the value of the meal you would have paid for, and many diners do, since the server does the same work. This post tips on the menu price in the examples. If you tip on the lower price, your saving is a little larger, and you can decide what feels right. Whatever you decide, put it into your tally so the number stays honest.

What a month looks like

Four weeks, three lunches out a week (example)
  • Without any deal: 12 lunches × $15.36 = $184.32.
  • With a $3 off deal on 4 of them: 4 × $3.39 = $13.56 saved.
  • With a 2 for 1 on 4 of them: 4 × $6.78 = $27.12 saved.
  • Both together, on 8 of the 12 lunches: $13.56 + $27.12 = $40.68 saved, leaving $143.64.

These are example numbers. Your lunches, your prices and your tip will differ, but you can redo the sums in two minutes. A monthly eating-out budget shows how to fit lunches into a bigger number.

How to find a lunch deal on FIG

FIG is free for diners. You can browse places, menus, reviews and live deals on a map without an account. When you find one you want, make an account, claim it in one tap and show the QR code that staff scan at the restaurant. A few rules help to know:

  • A diner can claim a deal once a day.
  • A deal code works once. If the restaurant lets a deal be used more than once, your screen shows a new code after each scan.
  • Location is optional and never limits a deal.

Check the hours of the deal. A lunch deal that ends at 2 pm is no use at 2:15. If you are not sure, how to compare restaurant deals helps you decide whether it is worth it.

Takeout at lunch

If you would rather pick lunch up, takeout on FIG is paid for online in the app at checkout. The card is held and charged when the restaurant accepts, and nothing is charged if the restaurant declines. There is no paying at the counter, and the pickup code only confirms collection. Takeout comes with a deal unless the restaurant has set takeout at full price, so check the total at checkout. Takeout or dine-in with a deal compares the two.

Small habits that add up

  • Look at the map on the morning of a lunch out, not at noon when you are hungry.
  • Ask a colleague on the days you want a 2 for 1.
  • Keep one lunch a week as a treat, with no deal at all. A budget you enjoy is a budget you keep.
