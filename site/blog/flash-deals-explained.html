What are flash deals, and how do they work on FIG?

Flash deals are short, real-time restaurant deals posted during unexpected quiet spells. Here's how they work for diners and restaurants, how to catch them, and what happens to a claim when the timer ends.

Key takeaways

  • A flash deal is a short deal a restaurant posts right now, usually for an hour or two, because it has a quiet spell.
  • The timer is set by the restaurant and it's real: when it ends, the deal is gone.
  • Live flash deals stand out on the map and on Home, with their timer, and the Flash deals filter shows only them.
  • A claimed flash deal works until the timer ends, then it expires. Expired claims never count against you.
  • Restaurants post one in seconds, with an optional cap.

The short answer

A flash deal is a short restaurant deal posted on the spot, because the restaurant has empty tables right now. It runs for a set time, usually an hour or two, with a real countdown. Claim it while it's live, head over, and show your code before the timer ends.

How to catch one

Live flash deals stand out on the map and on Home, with their timer, and the Flash deals filter shows only them. Because they're short, the best way to catch them is to check FIG when you're already thinking about food, the same moment you'd open a group chat to ask "where should we eat?".

Claim it, then go

  1. Tap the flash deal and Claim it. It's on your Deals page with the time left.
  2. Head over. A claim doesn't need you to be anywhere in particular: you can claim from home.
  3. At the counter, tap Show QR and let staff scan it.

A code works once, so a screenshot of a used code can't be reused. If the timer runs out before you get there, the claim expires. It shows as Expired on your Deals page for a day, and it never counts against you.

Are flash deals real?

Yes, and it's worth saying why. On FIG, the timer is the restaurant's own: the owner chose the deal and how long it runs, because they actually have the space. FIG doesn't invent urgency, and a flash deal isn't a fake countdown that resets tomorrow.

For restaurant owners

Posting a flash deal takes a few seconds: pick the deal (a percentage off, a dollar amount off, 2-for-1 or something free), the length and an optional cap. There's no fee per deal or per scan. Good flash deals are:

  • Simple. One dish or one clear discount, readable at a glance.
  • Short. Long enough for someone nearby to get there, short enough to stay special.
  • Capped. So a sudden rush can't overwhelm the kitchen.
  • Real. Post one when you genuinely have room, and diners learn to trust them.

The life of a flash deal

2:41 pmIt stands out on the map and on Home, and shows on the restaurant's page, with the time left.
2:45 to 4:10 pmDiners nearby claim it and head over. Each claim waits on their Deals page.

Why restaurants have surprise quiet spells

A dining room can go quiet for reasons nobody planned: a downpour, a cancelled group booking, a slow day after a holiday weekend, a road closure out front. The kitchen is staffed and the food is prepped either way. A flash deal turns that spare capacity into diners, and because it's short and capped, it doesn't train people to wait for discounts.

Flash deal etiquette for diners

  • Claim it when you mean to go. Caps are small, and an unused claim may keep someone else out.
  • Go promptly. The deal ends with the timer, wherever you are.
  • Check dine-in or takeout before you set off.
  • Like the deal afterwards if it was good: it tells the restaurant, and other diners, that it worked.

Is a flash deal worth it?

A $15 bowl with a food cost of $4.50 still leaves $6.50 with $4 off. In an hour where those seats would sit empty, fifteen of them add up to $97.50 toward the shift's wages and rent, before any drinks. Use your own numbers. The full slow-hour math.

Questions

How long do flash deals last?

The restaurant sets the length, usually an hour or two. The timer you see in the app is the real one.

What happens if my flash deal runs out before I arrive?

The claim expires when the timer ends. It shows as Expired on your Deals page for a day, then leaves the list, and it never counts against you.

Will FIG message me about flash deals?

Not on its own. Check the map or Home, or turn on the Flash deals filter to see only them.

Do I have to be nearby to claim a flash deal?

No. Deals are never locked to your location. Just make sure you can get there before the timer ends.

Can a restaurant post a flash deal for takeout?

A restaurant chooses whether each deal is dine-in only or also takeout.

Do flash deals cost restaurants anything?

No. Posting deals and scanning diners are free, with no fee per deal or per diner.
