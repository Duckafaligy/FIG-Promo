Live now or scheduled: when to start a deal on FIG

A deal can go live the moment you post it or start later. When each makes sense, how uses per diner and the wait between uses shape the week, and a worked example that checks what a $3-off deal really costs.

Key takeaways

  • On FIG a deal can be live now or scheduled, so you choose when diners first see it.
  • Post live now when you want to fill a quiet hour today. Schedule when you can prepare staff first.
  • Uses per diner and the wait between uses decide how often one person can come back for the same deal.
  • A diner can claim a deal once a day, and a deal code works once, so one deal cannot be used over and over by accident.
  • Check the cost with real arithmetic: in the example below a $3-off deal on a $15 meal nets $66 across 20 diners.

The short answer

Post a deal live now when you want to fill a quiet hour today, and schedule it when you want your team ready first, or when the deal belongs to a day later in the week. Either way, decide how many times one diner can use it and how long they wait between uses. Then check the cost with your own numbers before you post. FIG is completely free for restaurants to set up and run, so you can change timing as often as you like.

What live now and scheduled mean

When you post a deal on FIG you choose when it starts. Live now means diners can see and claim it straight away. Scheduled means you set it up ahead of time and it starts when you chose. Both kinds can be a 2 for 1, a percent off, a dollar off or a free item with a dish.

Live nowScheduled
When diners see itStraight awayWhen it starts
Best forA slow afternoon that is already happeningA quiet hour you can see coming
Staff readinessTell the team before you postBrief the team any time before the start
RiskPosting in a rush, with the wrong numbersForgetting a deal is set, then closing early

The deal itself is the same. What changes is how much time you have to think, and how much time your staff have to get ready.

When to go live now

Some quiet hours cannot be predicted. A storm clears the street, a school event finishes early, or the lunch rush simply does not show. If the room is half empty and you have the stock, a live-now deal is the fastest response you have. It costs nothing to post, and you can end it as soon as the room fills.

A few habits keep a same-day deal safe:

  • Tell the person at the till before you post, so the first diner is not a surprise. Train your staff to honour a FIG deal covers a ten-minute briefing.
  • Pick a deal you can price in your head, such as a dollar off a dish you already sell well.
  • Check you have the ingredients for the dishes the deal touches.

When to schedule

Most quiet hours are predictable. You probably know the day and the hour when the room thins out. A scheduled deal lets you set it once, when you have a calm moment, and have it start on its own.

  • You want the team briefed. Schedule the deal for tomorrow and tell the morning shift today.
  • You are testing. Start with one scheduled deal for one slow hour, and read the results before you add another. Reading your deal results shows how.
  • The deal has a purpose on a certain day. A quiet weekday lunch is a different problem from a slow Sunday evening, and each can have its own deal.

When a schedule changes, such as a holiday closure, change or end the deal so a diner does not claim something you cannot honour. Keeping your page current has a weekly routine for this.

Uses per diner and the wait between uses

Each deal has settings for how many times one diner can use it, and an optional wait between uses. A deal can be used once, or a restaurant can let it be used more than once, up to 20 times, with a wait between uses if you want one. After each scan, the diner's screen shows a new code. A deal code works once.

A diner can claim a deal once a day, so even a generous deal does not mean the same person arrives three times in an afternoon. Think about what you want:

GoalSetting to consider
Bring a new diner in onceOne use per diner
Build a habit for a weekday lunchSeveral uses with a wait, such as a week, between them
Protect a thin marginOne use, or a small number of uses with a longer wait

Check the cost before you post

A dollar-off deal is simple, which makes it easy to check. Here is an example with round numbers. Use your own prices and food cost.

A $3-off deal on a $15 meal, checked (example)
  • Your average meal is $15 and costs you about 30% to make, so $4.50 of food.
  • Suppose 20 diners use the deal in a month. Say 12 would not have come without it, and 8 would have come anyway.
  • The 12 new diners each pay $15 − $3 = $12. After $4.50 of food, each leaves $7.50. Twelve of them leave $90.
  • The 8 who would have come anyway each get $3 off a meal they would have paid full price for, so that is $24 given up.
  • Net: $90 − $24 = $66.

Change the split and the answer moves. If only 4 of the 20 were new, you would have $30 from them and $48 given up on the other 16, so the net is a loss of $18. These are example numbers, not a forecast. They show why a deal belongs in your quiet hours, when the people it brings in would otherwise be empty seats.

The slow-hour math works through the same idea with other deal types.

A weekly rhythm

A simple pattern is one live-now deal as needed, plus one or two scheduled deals aimed at your known slow hours.

  1. On the same day each week, look at the quiet hours you had.
  2. Schedule a deal for the next one that fits.
  3. Keep one deal idea ready to post live now if the room is empty.
  4. After two weeks, check the results and keep, change or stop each deal.

Keep it small. One deal that you understand is worth more than five that you cannot keep track of.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Posting without telling staff. The first diner should not be the first time the team hears of it.
  • Leaving a scheduled deal set through a closure. Check it when you change your hours.
  • Setting uses too high on day one. You can raise the limit later. It is harder to take a deal back from diners who have seen it.
  • Pricing from memory. Write down the dish price, the food cost and the deal, then do the sum.

Questions

Can I post a FIG deal that starts later?

Yes. A deal can be live now or scheduled, so you can set it up ahead of time.

How many times can one diner use a deal?

That is up to you. A deal can be used once, or a restaurant can let it be used more than once, up to 20 times, with an optional wait between uses.

Can a diner claim the same deal again and again in one day?

No. A diner can claim a deal once a day, and a deal code works once.

What happens to the code after a diner uses a deal more than once?

The diner's screen shows a new code after each scan. A deal code works once.

Does it cost anything to post or schedule a deal?

No. Setting up your page, going live, posting deals, scanning and the dashboard cost nothing for restaurants.

What types of deal can I schedule?

A 2 for 1, a percent off, a dollar off, or a free item with a dish.

How do I know whether a deal worked?

Your dashboard shows results deal by deal. Read them after a couple of weeks and decide to keep, change or stop the deal.
