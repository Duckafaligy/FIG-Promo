The short answer

Look at the live deals on the map, claim one in a tap when you know when you will eat, and show your QR code to the staff, who scan it. A diner can claim a deal once a day, so a week of eating out can use a different deal each day. In the example below, three deals across one week save $14 before tax and tip. FIG is free for diners, and you can check out deals at www.figpromo.ca/app.

How a claim works

Guests can look around, but you need an account to claim, order, save or review. Diners must be 14 or older. When you find a deal, you claim it in one tap and show a QR code that staff scan at the restaurant.

  • A deal code works once.
  • If a restaurant lets a deal be used more than once, up to 20 times with an optional wait between uses, your screen shows a new code after each scan.
  • Location is optional, and it never limits a deal.
  • Restaurants never see your phone number.

Why once a day matters

A claim a day is a useful limit. It stops you from using up a good deal on a snack, and it nudges you to choose. If two good deals are live on the same day, you can claim one, and the other is there tomorrow if the restaurant still has it live.

Think of the daily claim as a menu for the week, not a race. A good habit is to look at the map on a Sunday evening, pick the two or three days you expect to eat out, and claim only on those days. Claiming a deal you then do not use is a small waste of your own day's claim.

A week, as an example

This is an example week with round numbers. Your deals, prices and appetite will be different. Savings are before HST and tip, and we assume the deal is taken in full.

Three deals in one week (example)
DayDealMealYou save
Monday$3 off a lunchA $13 lunch, so $10 before tax$3
Wednesday2 for 1 with a colleague, two $14 mainsPay $14 for the pair, so $7 each$7 for you
Friday$4 off a dinnerA $19 dinner, so $15 before tax$4
Total for you$3 + $7 + $4 = $14

Check: the 2 for 1 saves $14 in all, because one of the two $14 mains is free, and half of that is $7 each. Your three meals would have cost $13 + $14 + $19 = $46 at the menu prices, so $14 off is about 30%. A deal does not make a meal free. It makes the meal you were going to have a bit cheaper.

Deals you can use more than once

Some restaurants let a deal be used more than once, with an optional wait between uses. If a place you like does this, you do not need to claim every time you want a meal. After each scan your screen shows a new code, so the same code cannot be reused by anyone else.

It still helps to think ahead. Check the rules on the deal: how many uses are left and how long you have to wait. A wait between uses is there so that a deal supports regular visits without being used up in one afternoon.

Check the deal before you claim

  • The dish. Is it something you would order anyway? A deal on a dish you do not want is not a saving.
  • The days and hours. A restaurant sets when a deal runs. Check you can get there.
  • The reviews. Reviews come only from scanned visits, so they are from real diners. Deals get likes, and restaurants get stars.
  • The extras. Drinks, tax and tip are still yours to pay.

Eating out with other people

Deals are easier to use with friends. A 2 for 1 needs two people. A dollar-off deal works for each person who claims it, since each diner claims their own. Agree on the place before you leave, and each person claims on their own phone. Because a code works once, the person who claimed it is the one who shows it.

Two friends, two different deals (example)
  • Friend A claims $3 off a $12 bowl and pays $9.00 before tax.
  • Friend B claims $4 off a $16 plate and pays $12.00 before tax.
  • Together: $9.00 + $12.00 = $21.00, against $28.00 at menu prices. That is $7.00 saved between them.

These are example prices before tax and tip. If you each claim in the app and agree to keep the bill simple, the restaurant handles it as it normally would.

When a deal is not worth it

A deal is not always the right choice. Skip it if the dish is one you would not otherwise order, if the trip costs more than the saving, or if the only deal available is on a day you cannot go. The point of a deal is to nudge you toward somewhere you already wanted to eat, not to change what you want. A $4 saving on a 40-minute round trip is a poor trade. A $4 saving at the place in your own plaza is a good one.

If you want to eat at home

Some deals can be used for takeout. You pay online in the app at checkout, your card is held and charged when the restaurant accepts, and nothing is charged if the restaurant declines. Ordering takeout on FIG, step by step goes through it.

A routine that takes five minutes

  1. On Sunday, open the map and look at the deals for the days ahead.
  2. Pick the one or two you would really use, and note the days.
  3. On the day, claim the deal before you leave, so you are not fiddling at the counter.
  4. Show your QR code and let the staff scan it.
  5. After the meal, leave a review if you want. Reviews come from scanned visits.
