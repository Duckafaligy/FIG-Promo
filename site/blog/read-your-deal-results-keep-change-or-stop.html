Reading your deal results: keep, change or stop a deal

Your dashboard shows results deal by deal. How to review a deal after two weeks, three questions to ask, and a worked example of the arithmetic that tells you whether to keep it, change it or end it.

Key takeaways

  • Your FIG dashboard shows results deal by deal, so you can judge each deal on its own.
  • Give a deal at least two weeks and change one thing at a time.
  • Ask three questions: did the quiet window fill, who used the deal, and what did each use leave you?
  • Your own till tells you how many diners would have come anyway. The dashboard cannot know that for you.
  • At the end of a review, keep, change or stop the deal, and write down the date and the reason.

The short answer

After a deal has run for two weeks, open your dashboard, read the results for that one deal, and compare them with your own till for the same hours. Ask whether the quiet window filled, whether new faces came, and what each use left you after food cost. Then do one of three things: keep the deal, change one setting, or stop it.

Why review a deal at all

A deal is a bet on your quiet hours. Some bets pay off, some break even and some cost money without anyone noticing. The difference is easy to see if you look, and easy to miss if you do not. Looking takes ten minutes every two weeks, which is less time than it takes to make one batch of dough.

What you have to look at

SourceWhat it shows you
FIG dashboardResults deal by deal, including how many diners used each deal
Your till or sales reportHow busy the same hours were before the deal, and during it
Your kitchen notesWhether the rush was manageable or whether the deal swamped the line
Your reviewsWhat diners said about the visit. You can reply to them in the app

The dashboard tells you what the deal did. Only your own records can tell you what would have happened without it.

Give it two weeks

A single day proves nothing. A rainy Tuesday, a school holiday or a road closure can change a lunch hour more than any deal can. Two full weeks, with the same hours each week, gives you at least two samples of each weekday. If you can wait four weeks, better still.

During the test, do not change the deal, the hours or the price. If you change three things, you will not know which one made the difference.

Three questions to ask

  1. Did the quiet window fill? Compare the hours of the deal with the same hours before it. A deal can have many uses and still leave the room just as empty if the uses were all in an hour that was already busy.
  2. Who used it? Regulars who would have come at full price, or new diners? Staff will usually know. If your regulars are the ones using it, the deal is a discount, not a draw.
  3. What did each use leave you? This is the arithmetic, shown below.

A worked example

These are round example numbers, not a real restaurant. A $15.00 dish costs $4.50 to make (a food cost of 30%). A deal takes $3.00 off, and the deal is dine-in, so FIG takes nothing from the bill. Over two weeks the dashboard shows 40 uses. Your till and your staff tell you that about 25 of those diners would have come anyway, and 15 would not.

Two weeks of a $3 off deal (example)
LineWorkingAmount
Left from a dish at the deal price$15.00 − $3.00 − $4.50$7.50
Left from the same dish at full price$15.00 − $4.50$10.50
Cost of the 25 who would have come anyway25 × $3.00− $75.00
Earned from the 15 new diners15 × $7.50+ $112.50
Net for the two weeks$112.50 − $75.00+ $37.50

The deal is positive here, but only by a little, and the result depends heavily on the 25-and-15 split. If 30 had come anyway and 10 were new, the cost is 30 × $3.00 = $90.00 and the gain is 10 × $7.50 = $75.00, a net of − $15.00. The same deal flips from a gain to a loss. That is why the split matters more than the total.

Keep, change or stop

What you seeTry
Quiet window filled, net positiveKeep it. Consider more uses per diner, with a wait between uses
Many uses, window already busyMove the deal to a quieter window
Mostly regularsNarrow the hours, lower the discount or change the deal type
Few usesCheck the photo, the dish and the hours. Then try another dish before cutting the discount
Too many uses for the kitchenShorten the hours or add a limit. For takeout, use pause and prep time
Net negative after two weeksStop it, and write down what you learned

Uses per diner and the wait between uses explains the two settings, and the deal-type guide helps when it is the type that needs to change.

Change one thing at a time

If a deal needs fixing, change a single setting and run it for another two weeks. The settings you can change are the discount, the dish, the days and hours, the uses per diner and the wait between uses. Posting a deal live now or scheduled is also up to you, so a change can be set to start on a Monday.

Keep a notes page with a line for each change: the date, what you changed, why and what happened. In three months you will be glad of it.

Share the numbers with your team

The people who work the quiet shift know things the dashboard does not: which regulars came, whether the new faces ordered a drink, whether the room felt busier. Ask them two questions at the end of the two weeks: who used the deal, and what did you notice? Write the answers next to your numbers. A thirty-second talk at the end of a shift is often the most useful part of the review, and it also shows the team that the deal is something they have a say in.

Do not forget the reviews

Numbers do not show how a diner felt. Read what diners say about their visits, and reply. A review that says the line was too long tells you more than a use count can. Reviews come only from scanned visits, so each one is from someone who really came. Replying to reviews shows how.

Before FIG opens to diners

Diners can use FIG from January 1, 2027. Until then there are no results to read, since diners can only join the waitlist at www.figpromo.ca. Use the weeks before to build deals you can judge later: choose a quiet window, do the arithmetic from the slow-hour math and write down your guess for how many new diners it will bring. In January, you will have something to compare it with.

Questions

Where do I see how a deal is doing?

Your dashboard shows results deal by deal.

How long should I run a deal before judging it?

At least two weeks, with the same hours each week, and without changing the deal while you wait.

Can the dashboard tell me whether a diner would have come anyway?

No. Only your own till, your staff and your regulars can tell you that, which is why the arithmetic uses your own numbers.

What should I change first if a deal is not working?

Change one thing at a time: the hours, the dish, the discount, the uses per diner or the wait between uses.

How do I know whether a deal pays?

Compare its results on your dashboard with your own till, then do the arithmetic on what each use leaves you after food cost.

Do restaurants see diners' phone numbers?

No. Restaurants never see diners' phone numbers.

Can I read results before FIG opens to diners?

Not yet. FIG opens to diners on January 1, 2027, and until then diners can only join the waitlist at www.figpromo.ca.
