Is FIG Premium worth $50 a year? A break-even check for restaurants

FIG Premium is $50 CAD a year. Here is what it includes, the break-even sum in plain numbers, and who should skip it, so you can decide with your own receipts.

Key takeaways

  • FIG Premium is $50 CAD a year, about $4.17 a month. Posting deals on FIG is free, with no commission on deals.
  • Premium includes unlimited locations, deal announcements every 2 hours, area insights, a menu typed up from a photo and a Premium badge.
  • On a $20 dish with $4 off, a new diner in an empty hour leaves about $10 after a 30% food cost. Five of those cover the year.
  • Premium is worth most to owners with several locations or a menu they would rather not type in by hand.
  • If you have one location, a short menu and no wish for the extras, you can post deals for free and see for yourself.

The short answer

FIG Premium costs $50 CAD a year, which is about $4.17 a month. It is optional: restaurants post deals on FIG for free, with no commission on deals, and Premium adds extras on top. For a single-location restaurant with a short menu, it may not be needed at first. For an owner with several locations, or a long menu to enter, it can pay for itself quickly.

This post lists what Premium includes, does the break-even sum with round example numbers you can redo with your own, and says who should skip it. Nothing here promises a number of new diners. Only your own receipts can tell you that.

What Premium includes

What you get without Premium: your page, your menu, your deals, scanning diners at the counter and the diners' reviews, with no commission on deals. The rest of this post compares the two so you can see whether the extras are worth $50 to you. Exact screens and wording are in the restaurant side of the app, so check Settings for the current details before you buy.

The break-even sum

The question to ask is simple: how many extra visits does it take to pay back $50? Use a dish you would really sell at a quiet hour. This example uses round numbers; swap in your own menu price and food cost.

Example: a $20 dish with a $4-off deal
Amount
Menu price$20.00
Deal−$4.00
The diner pays for the dish$16.00
Food cost at 30% of the menu price ($20.00)−$6.00
Left toward wages, rent and profit$10.00
Premium for a year$50.00
Visits that cover it: $50 ÷ $105

Five extra diners in a year, in hours that would otherwise have been empty, cover the whole cost. That works out to roughly one extra visit every ten weeks (52 weeks ÷ 5 visits is about 10.4). The 30% food cost is a round example, and a quoted industry range is 28% to 32%.

The catch is the word "extra". A diner who would have come anyway and used the deal did not add a visit. They only cost you the $4. This is why we suggest reading your insights per deal: claims, scans, and who came back. The slow-hour math covers how to tell the difference.

If you own more than one location

Unlimited locations is where the cost per place drops. Split the $50 across your locations:

Example: $50 across three locations
  • $50 ÷ 3 = $16.67 per location per year.
  • That is about $1.39 a month per location ($16.67 ÷ 12).
  • With four locations it is $12.50 each, about $1.04 a month.

If you run several restaurants, the larger saving is usually time: one sign-in for all of them, and the same menu approach across every page. Even so, check whether each location needs its own deals. A deal that works at a lunch spot near offices may not help a family restaurant on a different street, and each page needs its own hours, photos and dishes.

If typing in your menu is the hard part

The menu feature is a time saver. You photograph your paper menu, FIG types it up, and you check it before you publish. Only you know whether it is worth $50. As an example, if typing a 60-dish menu would take you an hour and your time is worth $25 an hour, that is $25 of your time once, and again every time the menu changes. Either way, you still review every price before it goes live. Nothing changes for diners until you publish. How the setup works in 15 minutes.

If your menu is ten dishes, or you already have it typed in a document, you may not need this.

A fair test: write down how long your last menu update took, from the first price you changed to the moment it was correct everywhere. If the answer is "a few minutes", skip the feature. If it is "an evening, and I still found a typo a week later", the photo route is worth a look.

What about the badge and the announcements?

We would rather be honest than make promises. A badge is a label on your page, and an announcement is a way to say "we have a deal" more often. Neither is a promise of more diners. Reviews on FIG still come only from scanned visits, so a badge cannot change what diners say about you. Announcements are best kept for deals that are really on. A restaurant that announces a flash deal when it truly has empty tables builds trust. One that announces every two hours with nothing new does the opposite. How flash deals work.

Who should skip it

  • A single restaurant with a short menu that wants to try deals first. Posting is free, so start there and look at your insights after two weeks.
  • Owners who have not found a deal that works. A better deal, hour or cap will do more than any extra. Choosing a deal type.
  • Anyone who is unsure about the budget. $50 is small, but a free option exists, so there is no pressure.

Who should consider it

  • Owners with two or more locations, where one price covers them all.
  • Owners with a long menu and a paper copy.
  • Owners who want to announce deals more often and have the quiet hours to justify it.
  • Owners who like to compare their numbers with the area around them.

A sensible way to decide: run your first deals free for a month, note how many scans came from new diners, and do the $50 sum with your own dish and food cost. If you cover it in a handful of visits, it is an easy yes. If not, wait.

You can also revisit the decision later. Nothing about posting deals changes if you start without Premium, and your page, menu and deals stay as they are.

Taxes and receipts

As with any business expense, keep the receipt and ask your accountant how it is treated in your books. This is general information, not tax advice. If you also take takeout paid in the app, that is a different cost: FIG keeps 5% of the food before tax, plus Stripe's card fee. Takeout order math on four order sizes.

Questions

How much is FIG Premium?

$50 CAD a year, which is about $4.17 a month.

Do restaurants have to pay to post deals on FIG?

No. Restaurants post deals free, with no commission on deals. Premium is optional.

What does FIG Premium include?

Unlimited locations, deal announcements every 2 hours, area insights, a menu typed up from a photo and a Premium badge.

Is FIG Premium worth it for one restaurant?

It depends on your menu and your plans. The sum in this post shows how many extra visits cover the year. If you have a short menu and one location, you can start with the free version.

Does Premium bring more diners?

FIG cannot promise a number of diners. Check your insights for claims, scans and returning diners, and do the break-even sum with your own prices.

Does the Premium badge change my reviews?

No. Reviews on FIG come only from diners whose visit was scanned.

Can I use Premium for several locations?

Yes. Premium covers unlimited locations for one price.
