Before you go: a two-minute check of a restaurant on FIG

A restaurant's FIG page shows hours, a menu, reviews and live deals in one place. Here is a short checklist to run before you claim a deal and head out.

The short answer

Before you go, check five things on the restaurant's FIG page: its opening hours, its menu, its reviews, the exact wording and times of the live deal, and whether takeout is on. It takes about two minutes, and it saves you from the most common letdowns: a closed kitchen, a deal that does not cover your dish, or a deal that is not live at the hour you arrive.

FIG is free for diners and open on the web at www.figpromo.ca/app. Home works as a guest, so you can look without an account.

1. Opening hours

Check that the restaurant is open when you expect to arrive, and that the kitchen is serving. Hours can change for holidays, so look at the day you are going, not just the usual hours. A restaurant keeps its own hours up to date, which is why you can trust the page more than an old note. If you run into a closed door anyway, what to check when a deal is not working is a calm place to start.

2. The menu

A restaurant's menu on FIG has photos and sections. Look for the dish you want, its price and anything that fits your needs. If you have an allergy or a dietary need, the page is a starting point, and it is always best to ask the restaurant directly before you eat. Eating out with dietary needs or allergies lists what to ask.

Seeing prices first also lets you work out what a deal is worth to you before you leave home.

3. Reviews

Reviews on FIG come only from scanned visits, so each one is from a person who was actually there. Deals get likes, and restaurants get stars. Skim the most recent reviews for what people say about the dish you want, and for anything about waiting times or portion sizes. A single bad review is a single bad evening, so look for repeated comments. How to read a restaurant's reviews on FIG goes through it.

4. The deal's wording, days and hours

This is the check that saves the most. Each restaurant sets its own deal, days and hours, so read them rather than assuming. A deal might be a 2 for 1, a percent off, a dollar off or free with a dish. It may cover only certain dishes or only a quiet hour.

Two deals on the same order (example)

Say two of you order two mains at $18 each and two drinks at $4 each, so $36 + $8 = $44.00 before tax. These are round example numbers.

Check: $18 + $8 = $26.00, and 20% of $44 is $8.80, so $35.20. The same order costs $9.20 less with the 2 for 1 in this example, but a different order, such as one main and a starter, could flip it. A deal's size matters less than what it covers.

How to compare restaurant deals has more ways to size one up.

5. Takeout, if you want it

If you would rather eat at home, check whether the restaurant has takeout on. When it does, you pay online in the app at checkout. The card is held and charged when the restaurant accepts, and nothing is charged if the restaurant declines. There is no paying at the counter, and the pickup code only confirms collection. Takeout comes with a deal unless the restaurant sets 0 deals per order, in which case it is at full price.

See ordering takeout on FIG for the steps.

How long it should take

Two minutes is a guide, not a rule. If you already know the place, the check is a glance at the hours and the deal. If it is somewhere new, spend a little longer on the menu and the reviews. Either way, do it before you leave home, since it is easier to change your mind from your sofa than from the car park.

Then claim it

When the page looks right, claiming takes a moment:

  1. Make an account if you do not have one yet. Guests can look around but need an account to claim, order, save or review. Diners must be 14 or older.
  2. Tap to claim the deal. You can claim a deal once a day.
  3. At the restaurant, show the QR code. Staff scan it. A deal code works once, and if the restaurant lets a deal be used more than once, your screen shows a new code after each scan.

Restaurants never see your phone number. What a restaurant sees when you use FIG explains what is shared and what is not.

If something looks off

Sometimes a page and the real world disagree. The hours on the page may differ from what is on the door, or a dish in the menu may be sold out. Restaurants keep their own pages, so a small mismatch is usually a change nobody has made yet rather than anything deliberate. Be kind about it, ask the staff what is available, and if a deal does not seem to apply, ask politely before you pay. A restaurant that sees a gap between its page and the door can fix it the same day, and a short, friendly word from you helps.

If you cannot sort it out at the table, you can reach FIG on the contact page.

A quick checklist to keep

  • Hours: open when I arrive, with the kitchen on.
  • Menu: the dish I want is there, at a price I know.
  • Reviews: recent, repeated comments, not one bad night.
  • Deal: wording, days and hours match when I am going.
  • Takeout: on or off, as I prefer.
  • Claim: done, QR code ready.

Location is optional on FIG and never limits a deal, so the checklist works from your couch or from the car park. If you are comparing a few places, how to find the best restaurant deals in Markham helps you narrow down the list.
