Your first week live on FIG: a day-by-day routine for restaurants

Going live is the start, not the finish. A simple seven-day routine for your first week on FIG: check your page, brief your team, post a first deal, scan, read the numbers and decide what to do next.

Key takeaways

  • Setting up, going live, posting deals, scanning and the dashboard cost nothing for restaurants.
  • Spend day 1 on your page as a diner sees it: hours, menu and photos.
  • Brief your team before the first deal, and give them a short script for scanning.
  • Start with one deal aimed at one quiet hour, and read the results before adding more.
  • A 12-scan example week shows how to turn results into a keep, change or stop decision.

The short answer

In your first week, do one small thing a day. Check your page as a diner sees it, brief your team, post one deal for one quiet hour, scan the first diners, read the numbers and decide what to change. You do not need to do everything at once, and nothing in setup or running costs anything: FIG is completely free for restaurants.

Add it up: 20 + 15 + 10 + 10 + 10 + 15 = 80 minutes of set-aside work across the week, plus the time you spend on the floor anyway.

Day 1: look at your page as a diner would

Diners can browse places, menus, reviews and live deals on a map, even as guests. Open your page the way they would. Are your opening hours right for the coming week? Do the menu sections read in the order a diner would read them? Are prices the same as on the till, and do the photos match the plates? Keep your FIG page current has the routine, and menu photos with a phone helps if you need new ones.

Day 2: get the team ready

Staff join by accepting an invite from the owner, with the roles Owner, Manager and Staff. Send the invites, and brief the people who will be at the till. Owners and managers also need sign-in security for the money steps: an authenticator app or a code by email. FIG sends no texts. Setting up your team and sign-in security goes through it.

A short script for the till is enough: ask the diner to show their code, scan it, and serve the deal as written. Train your staff to honour a FIG deal in 10 minutes has the full version.

Day 3: post one deal

Pick the quiet hour that hurts most, and one deal for it. A deal can be a 2 for 1, a percent off, a dollar off or a free item with a dish, live now or scheduled, with uses per diner and a wait between uses. For a first deal, keep it simple: one use per diner, a dish you already sell well, and a discount you have worked out. Choosing between deal types compares them, and live now or scheduled helps with timing.

Day 4: watch the first scans

The first few scans are where small problems show up. Have a manager within reach. Each diner shows a QR code on their phone and staff scan it. A deal code works once. If the deal lets a diner use it more than once, the diner's screen shows a new code after each scan.

If a code does not scan, check the basics first. What to check and what to say was written for diners, and it is worth a read for staff as well.

Days 5 and 6: reviews and numbers

Reviews come only from scanned visits, so each one is from a diner who really came. Read new reviews, and reply to the ones that need it. Replying to reviews has a short guide. Then open the dashboard. Results are shown deal by deal, so you can see what each deal did.

Reading a first week (example)
  • Suppose a $3-off deal was scanned 12 times in the week, and your average bill before the deal was $15.
  • The bills before the deal were 12 × $15 = $180. The discount was 12 × $3 = $36, which is 20% of $180.
  • You took in $180 − $36 = $144.
  • With food at about 30% of the pre-deal price, food cost was 12 × $4.50 = $54. That leaves $144 − $54 = $90 before your other costs.

These are example numbers. One week is a small sample, so use it to spot problems, not to make big decisions. Check how many of the 12 were new faces to you.

Day 7: keep, change or stop

Ask three questions. Did the deal bring people in the quiet hour you chose? Did the numbers hold up? Did the team find it easy? Then pick one action. Keep it as it is, change one thing, such as the hours or the wait between uses, or stop it. Change one thing at a time so you know what caused a difference. Reading your deal results goes deeper after two weeks of data.

What to do if the first week is quiet

A slow first week is normal and it does not mean the deal failed. People need to find the page. Here are things you can do that cost nothing:

  • Tell your regulars at the till. A sentence such as "we have a deal on the FIG app this week" is enough.
  • Put the address of your page on your door or your counter, so people can look you up.
  • Check the deal is aimed at an hour when you really are quiet, and that it is live at the times you meant.
  • Make sure the photos and the menu look good. A diner choosing between two places will pick the one that looks ready.

Give a new deal two weeks before you judge it, and change one thing at a time.

Mistakes that cost an afternoon

  • Posting a deal and forgetting to tell the till. The first diner finds out the team has not heard of it.
  • Leaving old hours on the page. A diner arrives to a closed door and the review shows it.
  • Starting with three deals. You cannot tell which one worked.
  • Waiting for perfect. A page that is right and a deal that is simple beat a page that is still being polished.

If you have a second location, which FIG supports up to 2 for now, repeat the week for it with its own hours and menu.

If you offer takeout

Takeout is separate from your first week. A restaurant can turn takeout on only after it has added its HST number and finished Stripe payouts, and owners and managers need the stronger sign-in for those money steps. On takeout orders paid in the app, FIG keeps 5% of the food after any deal and before tax, and Stripe's card fee comes off the payment. There is no commission on the bill and none on dine-in. Setting up your menu and takeout has the steps.

Questions

Does it cost anything to be live on FIG?

No. Signing up, setting up the page, going live, posting deals, scanning and the dashboard cost nothing for restaurants.

How many deals should I start with?

One, for one quiet hour. Read the results, then add or change.

Who can use the scanner?

Staff who have joined by accepting an invite from the owner, with the roles Owner, Manager and Staff.

Do I need sign-in security straight away?

Owners and managers need an authenticator app or a code by email for money steps such as payouts, taking takeout live and refunds. FIG sends no texts.

How long should I run a deal before judging it?

A couple of weeks gives more to go on than one. The first week is for spotting problems.

Where do reviews come from?

Only from scanned visits, so each review is from a diner who came to your restaurant.

How many locations can I have?

Up to 2 locations for now.
