What a restaurant sees when you use FIG, and what stays private

Your phone number is never shared with a restaurant, location is optional, and browsing needs no account at all. A plain answer to what a deals app actually knows about you.

Key takeaways

  • Restaurants never see diners' phone numbers.
  • Browsing works as a guest. You need an account to claim, order, save or review.
  • Location is optional and never limits a deal, so a deal is not hidden because you turned it off.
  • A deal code works once, and your screen shows a new code after each scan of a deal you can use again.

The short answer

Restaurants never see diners' phone numbers. You can look at places, menus, reviews and live deals as a guest without an account at all, and location is optional and never limits a deal. What a restaurant gets is the scan of your code at its own counter, and your review if you write one.

Looking around costs you nothing in information

Home works as a guest, so browsing needs no account. You can look at places, menus, reviews and live deals on the map before you decide whether to sign up for anything. There is no step where you have to hand something over to see prices.

You need an account to claim a deal, order takeout, save a place or write a review, which makes sense: a claimed deal has to belong to someone, and a review has to be attached to a visit. Browsing FIG as a guest goes through the line between the two.

The short version is that FIG is built around one moment of contact: you show a code, someone scans it, you eat. The app does not need to put you and a restaurant in touch any other way.

Why location is optional

Plenty of apps make location the price of entry. On FIG it is optional, and it never limits a deal. If you leave it off, you can still see live deals on the map and claim them. If you turn it on, the map is simply more convenient because it starts where you are.

This matters more than it sounds. It means a deal cannot be withheld from you because you were in the wrong spot when you looked, and that you can plan a meal near work, near home or near wherever you will be that evening. Your code at the counter

When you claim a deal, you get a QR code that staff scan at the restaurant. A deal code works once. If the restaurant lets a deal be used more than once, up to 20 times with an optional wait between uses, your screen shows a new code after each scan.

  • A screenshot of a used code is no good to anyone, which is the point.
  • A diner can claim a deal once a day, so claims are yours to spread across the week.
  • The person who claimed the deal is the one who shows it, since the code belongs to that claim.

Using your daily claim works through a week of this, with the arithmetic.

Reviews and what they carry

Reviews come only from scanned visits, so a restaurant knows a reviewer came in, without needing your contact details. Deals get likes and restaurants get stars. Restaurants can reply to a review, and that reply is on the review, not sent to you some other way.

If you would rather not write anything, a like on a deal is still useful to the next diner. Writing a review on FIG covers what to put in one.

Signing in

FIG's sign-in security uses an authenticator app or a code by email. FIG sends no texts, so a sign-in code never arrives that way, and anything claiming to be one did not come from FIG.

Two small habits are worth having with any app that holds a payment card: use a password you do not use anywhere else, and turn on the extra sign-in step if you order takeout. On the restaurant side, owners and managers need it for money steps such as payouts, taking takeout live and refunds, which tells you how seriously that step is taken on FIG.

If you are signing in on a shared or borrowed phone, sign out when you are done. Your claims and your account belong to you, and a claimed deal is tied to the account that claimed it.

If you order takeout

Takeout on FIG is paid online in the app at checkout. Your card is held and charged when the restaurant accepts the order, and nothing is charged if the restaurant declines it. The kitchen starts when the restaurant accepts, and the pickup code only confirms collection.

So the restaurant is cooking against an order it has accepted, and you are not handing a card over at a counter. Ordering takeout on FIG, step by step follows the whole thing through, and takeout or dine-in compares what each costs you.

Why a deals app does not need much about you

It is worth saying why this works the way it does. A deal on FIG is a simple exchange: a restaurant offers something for a quiet hour, you decide whether you want it, and a member of staff scans a code when you arrive. None of that needs a restaurant to hold your contact details, and FIG is built so it does not.

The restaurant gets what it actually needs, which is the scan, the results of its own deals and its reviews. You get the deal, and you keep the rest. If you later stop using the app, you simply stop claiming. There is nothing running in the background that a restaurant has to be asked to switch off.

The short version to tell a friend

  1. You can look at everything without an account.
  2. Your phone number is never shared with a restaurant.
  3. Location is optional and never limits a deal.
  4. The restaurant scans a code that works once.
  5. Reviews come from visits that really happened.

That is most of what people want to know before they sign up for a deals app, and it is the same whether you use it once a month or twice a week.
