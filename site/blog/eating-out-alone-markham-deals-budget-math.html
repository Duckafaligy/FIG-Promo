Eating out on your own in Markham: lunch or dinner for one, with the math

Most deals are talked about for pairs and groups. Here is how to pick the right type of deal when you are eating alone, with a $20 meal worked out four ways.

The short answer

When you eat alone, pick a dollar-off or percent-off deal, or a deal that gives you something with your dish. A 2 for 1 gives you a second dish, and unless you want to eat two meals, that is a benefit you may not use. On a $20 meal, $4 off leaves you paying $16.00 and 25% off leaves you paying $15.00, before HST.

Why eating alone changes the math

Most advice about restaurant deals quietly assumes company. A 2 for 1 is great for two people, and a deal for a whole table is great for a family. When it is just you, the question becomes simpler: how much less do I pay for the one meal I will eat?

That is why it helps to count what you actually get. A deal that gives you a second dish you cannot finish is worth less to you than a deal that takes a few dollars off the one dish you order. If you have a friend who might join you another day, a 2 for 1 is still worth saving in your mind for then. For a table of several, eating out as a group goes through it.

One $20 meal, four deals

These are round example numbers, not any restaurant's menu. Say your meal is $20 before tax and you are eating alone.

A $20 solo meal, four deals (example)
DealYou pay (before HST)You save
No deal$20.00$0.00
$4 off$16.00$4.00
25% off$15.00$5.00
2 for 1, eating one dish$20.00$0.00 (the second dish is yours to take or share)
A $5 drink with your dish, with the deal$20.00$5.00 of value, paid for nothing extra

Check: $20 − $4 = $16. 25% of $20 is $5, so $20 − $5 = $15. For the 2 for 1 eaten by one person, you still pay $20 for the one dish you wanted. For the included drink, you pay $20 and receive $25 of menu items.

So alone, 25% off saves the most in this example, and the included drink is worth about the same if you would have bought that drink anyway. If you would not have, it saves you nothing.

What about HST?

This is general information, not tax advice. Ontario's HST is 13%. The Canada Revenue Agency says a store coupon reduces the price before GST/HST is calculated, so tax on a discounted bill is worked out on the lower price. Using the example above:

DealPriceHST at 13%You pay
No deal$20.00$2.60$22.60
$4 off$16.00$2.08$18.08
25% off$15.00$1.95$16.95

Check: 13% of $16 is $2.08, and 13% of $15 is $1.95. A restaurant's own till decides the final number, so treat these as examples. For the full picture, see how to compare restaurant deals.

How to claim a deal for one

  1. Look first. Browse places, menus, reviews and live deals on the map at www.figpromo.ca/app. You can do this as a guest, with no account.
  2. Read the deal. Each restaurant sets its own days, hours and wording, so check them before you head out. Location is optional and never limits a deal.
  3. Make an account to claim. Guests can look around, but you need an account to claim, order, save or review. Diners must be 14 or older.
  4. Claim in one tap. You can claim a deal once a day.
  5. Show your QR code. Staff scan it at the restaurant. A deal code works once, and if the restaurant lets a deal be used more than once, your screen shows a new code after each scan.

Making a week of solo meals

Because you can claim a deal once a day, a week of solo meals is a simple pattern: look at what is live, pick one meal a day, and claim only what you will use. Here is an example week, with made-up figures that you can swap for your own.

Example week of five weekday lunches
DayMenu priceDealYou pay
Monday$16.00$3 off$13.00
Tuesday$14.00No deal today$14.00
Wednesday$18.0025% off$13.50
Thursday$12.00$2 off$10.00
Friday$20.00$4 off$16.00

Menu prices add up to $16 + $14 + $18 + $12 + $20 = $80.00. What you pay is $13 + $14 + $13.50 + $10 + $16 = $66.50. The difference is $13.50 over the week, before HST. It is an example, not a promise of what any restaurant offers.

Choosing the place

Eating alone is easier in some places than others, and it comes down to what you like. A few things to look at on a restaurant's FIG page before you go:

  • Hours. Check that the kitchen is open when you expect to arrive.
  • The menu and its photos. You can see sections and prices before you walk in, which helps when you want a smaller dish.
  • Reviews. Reviews on FIG come only from scanned visits, so they are from people who were there. Deals get likes and restaurants get stars.
  • The deal's wording. If a deal is for two, a single claim may not suit you. Move on to the next one.

Takeout for one

Sometimes the best solo meal is eaten at home. If a restaurant has takeout on, you pay online in the app at checkout. The card is held and charged when the restaurant accepts, and nothing is charged if the restaurant declines. There is no paying at the counter, and the pickup code only confirms collection. Takeout comes with a deal unless the restaurant sets 0 deals per order, in which case takeout is at full price.

A short checklist

  • Decide what you want to eat, then look for a deal that fits it, not the other way round.
  • Prefer a dollar-off or percent-off deal when you are one person.
  • Check the days and hours in the deal, and the restaurant's opening hours.
  • Claim only what you will use today, since you can claim a deal once a day.
  • Have your QR code on screen when you ask for the bill.
  • Leave a review after a scanned visit, if you have something useful to say.

Questions

Is a 2 for 1 deal good if I am eating alone?

Usually not on its own, because it gives you a second dish. A dollar-off or percent-off deal often suits one person better. Read each deal's wording, since restaurants set their own.

Can I use a FIG deal when I eat by myself?

Yes. You claim a deal in one tap and show the QR code, and staff scan it. Check that the deal's wording does not need a second person.

How many deals can I claim in a day?

A diner can claim a deal once a day.

Do I need an account to look at deals?

No. Home works as a guest, so you can browse places, menus, reviews and live deals without an account. You need an account to claim, order, save or review.

Does FIG cost diners anything?

No. FIG is free for diners, and it is open on the web at www.figpromo.ca/app.

Do I have to share my location?

No. Location is optional and never limits a deal.

Can I order takeout for one with a deal?

If the restaurant has takeout on, yes. Takeout comes with a deal unless the restaurant sets 0 deals per order, and you pay in the app at checkout.
