Telling people about your FIG deal: a sign, a link and a staff script

A deal only works if people know it exists. Here is a short, practical way to tell regulars and passers-by: where to say it, what to say, and how to check it is working.

By the FIG team · For restaurants · November 19, 2026 · 5 min read

Key takeaways

  • Diners find deals on a map in the web app at www.figpromo.ca/app, but your own door, till and social pages are where regulars hear about it.
  • Keep the wording to one line: the deal, the hours, and where to claim it.
  • Give staff one sentence to say, and one thing to do if a guest has not claimed yet.
  • Browsing needs no account, but a guest needs an account to claim.
  • Check the dashboard after a week. Change one thing at a time.

The short answer

Put the deal where people already are: your door, your till and your own social pages. Say it in one line, with the hours and the web address www.figpromo.ca/app. Give your staff one sentence to say. Then look at your results after a week and change one thing at a time.

Why tell people yourself

FIG puts your deal on a map that diners browse, so new people can find you. But the people most likely to walk in during a quiet hour are the ones who already know your name: regulars, neighbours and the office next door. A short, clear word from you reaches them faster than any map.

It also helps with the quiet hours you are trying to fill. Seven ways to fill slow hours covers the deal itself; this post covers the telling.

Write one line first

Before any sign or post, write the deal in one line. It should say what it is, when it runs and where to claim it.

An example line (made up, use your own)

2 for 1 mains, Tuesday to Thursday, 2 pm to 5 pm. Claim at www.figpromo.ca/app and show your code when you order.

Every word in the line should match what you posted in the deal itself. If you change the deal, change the line. Writing a deal diners understand has wording that works.

Where to say it

WhereWhat to put thereWho does it
Front door or windowThe one line, with the hours and the web addressOwner or manager
Next to the tillThe same line, so a guest sees it while payingWhoever is on shift
On the table or menuA short note: "Ask us about today's deal"Front of house
Your own social pagesThe line, plus a link to your page on FIGOwner
Your receipt or bag, if you print themThe address, with the hours the deal runsOwner

Keep the wording the same everywhere. A deal described three different ways looks like three different deals.

What your staff should say

Staff are the most useful channel you have, and they need very little. One sentence is enough: "We have a deal on right now, you can claim it at figpromo.ca/app and show me the code."

  1. Say the sentence to a guest who is eating in the deal's hours.
  2. If they have not claimed, tell them they can look around without an account, but need one to claim. It takes a minute.
  3. If they have claimed, scan the code. It works once.
  4. If something fails, ask the guest to show the screen and call the manager. Train your staff to honour a FIG deal in 10 minutes goes through the scan.

Know the facts people ask

Guests will ask a few questions. Your team should be able to answer them:

  • What does it cost a diner? FIG is free for diners.
  • Do I need the app? FIG is open on the web at www.figpromo.ca/app. iPhone and Android apps are coming.
  • Can I use it twice? A diner can claim a deal once a day. If you let a deal be used more than once, up to 20 times, the diner's screen shows a new code after each scan.
  • Do you get my phone number? No. Restaurants never see diners' phone numbers.

Anything else, point them to your page on FIG or to the contact page.

Check whether it is working

After a week, open your dashboard. It shows results deal by deal. Put them next to what you did: when you put the sign up, when you started saying the line.

A simple check (example numbers)
  • Week 1, no sign: 6 scans on the Tuesday deal.
  • Week 2, sign and staff line: 11 scans.
  • Change: 11 − 6 = 5 more scans, which is 83% more (5 ÷ 6 = 0.83).

These are made-up numbers to show the sum. Your own two weeks are the real test.

Change one thing at a time, so you know what caused a difference. Reading your deal results has more.

A one-week routine

You do not need a campaign. A simple week looks like this:

  1. Monday. Write the one line. Check it matches the deal as posted.
  2. Tuesday. Put the line on the door and at the till. Tell your team the sentence.
  3. Wednesday to Friday. Say it to every guest in the deal's hours. Note how it lands.
  4. The weekend. Post the line on your own social pages with a link to your page on FIG.
  5. The next Monday. Open the dashboard and compare scans with the week before.

Keep a note of what you changed each day, even a line in a notebook by the till. When the dashboard shows a jump or a dip, the note tells you why, and you stop relying on memory.

Small and steady beats a big push once. If the week was quiet, change the hours before you change the discount. Live now or scheduled shows how.

Reaching the neighbours

Regulars are one group. The other is people who work or live within a short walk and have never come in. A few low-effort ways to reach them, all things you already control:

  • Your own social pages. Post the one line and the link to your page on FIG, and pin it for the length of the deal.
  • A word with nearby businesses. A short, friendly note to the shop next door, with the hours and the address, costs nothing.
  • Your page on FIG itself. Keep hours, photos and menu current, because a diner who finds you on the map will look at them before walking over. Keeping your page current covers it.

Do not guess at numbers for any of these. Try one for a week, look at the dashboard, and keep what works.

What to avoid

  • Promising what the deal does not say. If the deal ends at 5 pm, the sign says 5 pm.
  • Long signs. One line and an address. People read a door in two seconds.
  • Leaving an old sign up. If the deal ends or changes, take the sign down the same day.
  • Telling guests the discount is automatic. They need to claim it and show the code.

Questions

How do diners find my deal on FIG?

Diners browse places, menus, reviews and live deals on a map in the web app at www.figpromo.ca/app. Browsing needs no account.

What should a sign for my deal say?

One line: the deal, the days and hours, and where to claim it, which is www.figpromo.ca/app.

Do guests need an account to claim a deal?

Yes. Guests can look around, but need an account to claim, order, save or review.

What should staff say to a guest?

One sentence: there is a deal on right now, it can be claimed at figpromo.ca/app, and they should show the code when they order.

Can a diner use my deal more than once?

A diner can claim a deal once a day. If you allow more than one use, up to 20 times with an optional wait between uses, their screen shows a new code after each scan.

How do I know my sign worked?

Compare scans for the same deal before and after, using the dashboard. Change one thing at a time.

Does it cost anything to post a deal?

No. Posting deals, scanning and the dashboard cost nothing for restaurants.
