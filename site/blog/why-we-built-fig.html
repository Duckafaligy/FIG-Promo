Why we built FIG

Markham is full of great independent restaurants, and most of them run on thin margins. FIG is a deals app built around that: free for diners and for restaurants, with no cut of a dine-in bill.

By the FIG team · News · Updated October 25, 2026 · 4 min read

Key takeaways

  • FIG is a restaurant deals app starting in Markham, Ontario. Deals start January 1, 2027.
  • Restaurants post their own deals, for the hours they choose, with their own daily cap. Posting deals and scanning diners are free, with no fee per diner.
  • Diners find deals on a map, claim one in a tap and show a code at the counter. It's free for diners, with no ads for now.
  • FIG earns only 5% of the food on takeout orders paid in the app. Never from a dine-in bill.
  • Markham first, expanding to Richmond Hill and Vaughan Mills.

The short answer

We built FIG because the restaurants that make a city worth eating in are mostly small and independent, and the tools that promise them customers usually charge a share of every order. FIG turns that around: a restaurant posts a deal for the hours it wants to fill, nearby diners find it on a map, and the diner pays the restaurant at the counter. FIG takes nothing from that bill.

This post explains the thinking behind that model, what FIG does and doesn't do, and how it pays for itself without ads (for now) or commissions on dine-in.

Restaurants run on thin margins

Running a restaurant is a hard way to make money. Statistics Canada reported that in 2023 the operating profit margin for food services and drinking places in Canada fell to 3.6%, the lowest since 2003. Food and drink bought for resale was the largest expense, at 35.7% of operating costs, with wages close behind.

On a 3.6% margin, a $40 dinner leaves about $1.44 of profit. Anything that takes a percentage of the bill comes straight out of that, and usually more than all of it.

What a commission does to a thin margin
  • A $40 order at a 3.6% margin: about $1.44 of profit.
  • The same order on a 25% commission plan: $10.00 to the platform.
  • The commission is about seven times the profit the order would have made on its own.

Every restaurant's numbers are different, but the shape is the same: a percentage of the bill is paid before the restaurant has made a cent.

What delivery apps charge, and why that's a different job

Delivery platforms publish their Canadian rates. Uber Eats lists marketplace plans at 20%, 25% and 30% of each delivery order, and 10% on pickup orders. DoorDash lists 20%, 25% and 29% on delivery, and 10% or 8% on pickup, depending on the plan. Those rates pay for real work: drivers, the app, the marketplace and the customers it brings.

FIG isn't trying to replace delivery. It does a narrower job: getting people into a restaurant, or to the counter for a pickup, during the hours the owner chooses. For that job, a percentage of every bill isn't needed, so FIG doesn't charge one. Delivery app commissions vs FIG, with the numbers.

So we flipped the model

On FIG, a deal is a decision the owner made. The restaurant picks:

  • What the deal is: a percentage off, a dollar amount off, 2-for-1, or something free with an order, on the whole bill or on specific dishes from its menu.
  • When it runs: which days and hours, every week, once, or until a date. Or a flash deal right now, for a quiet hour.
  • How many: a daily cap, so a deal never gives away more than planned. It stops on its own when the cap is reached.
  • Where: dine-in only, or also as takeout.

A diner finds the deal on Home or on the map, taps Claim, and the deal waits on their Deals page. At the restaurant, staff scan the diner's code with the restaurant side of FIG (on any phone, tablet or computer). The deal comes off the bill, and the diner pays the restaurant the way they always do.

What FIG is for diners

  • Free. No subscription, no fees, no ads for now.
  • Deals you can actually use. Live deals near you, nearest first, filtered by what you crave, your budget and your diet.
  • No pressure. A claim doesn't time out. It works for as long as the deal runs, and it only ends when the restaurant ends the deal, or a flash or dated deal is over.
  • Private. Restaurants see your first name and last initial, never your phone number or email.
  • Honest reviews. Only diners whose visit was scanned can review a restaurant, so every review comes from a real meal. Deals get likes; restaurants get stars.

How FIG pays for itself

Restaurants use FIG for free. FIG earns in one way: on takeout paid in the app, it keeps 5% of the food (before tax), and Stripe's card fee comes off the same payment. The rest, tax included, goes to the restaurant.

That's it. No ads for now, no fee per diner, no commission on a dine-in bill, and nothing for sale that changes search order or reviews. How FIG makes money, in full.

Why Markham first

Because it's home, and because it has exactly the mix FIG is built for: hundreds of independent restaurants spread across plazas and main streets, from Pacific Mall at Steeles and Kennedy to Main Street Unionville and the old Main Street of Markham Village, and a lot of people who love finding somewhere new to eat.

FIG opens in Markham first, expanding to Richmond Hill and Vaughan Mills. Deals start January 1, 2027. Until then, restaurants can set up their page, menu and first deals, and diners can join the waitlist at www.figpromo.ca.

What's next

We're signing Markham restaurants now. If you own one, building your page, your menu and your first deal takes a few minutes, and it is free. To publish them, you finish Stripe's identity and bank checks. See how it works for restaurants or read the Help center.

Questions

What is FIG?

FIG is a restaurant deals app. Restaurants post their own deals; diners find them on a map, claim one and show a code at the counter. It's free for diners.

When does FIG launch?

FIG opens in Markham first, expanding to Richmond Hill and Vaughan Mills. Deals start January 1, 2027.

Does FIG take a commission?

Not on dine-in. Posting deals and scanning diners are free, with no fee per diner. On takeout orders diners pay for in the app, FIG keeps 5% of the food before tax.

Does FIG show ads?

No, not for now. FIG has no ads, and paying FIG never moves a restaurant up in search or on the map.

Does FIG deliver?

No. Diners eat in, or order takeout with a deal and pick it up.

Do I need the app to use FIG?

No. FIG runs in your web browser at www.figpromo.ca/app. Restaurants can set up there today; diners can join the waitlist on the site, and FIG opens to diners on January 1, 2027. The App Store and Google Play apps are coming soon.
