A five-minute end-of-day routine: matching your FIG scans to your till

Scans on your dashboard and discounts at your till should tell the same story. Here is a short closing routine, a worked example and what to do when the numbers differ.

By the FIG team · For restaurants · November 22, 2026 · 5 min read

Key takeaways

  • Compare two numbers at close: scans on the FIG dashboard and discounts rung in at your till.
  • In the example, 12 scans of a $3 off deal cost $36.00, which is 12.5% of $288.00 in menu-price sales.
  • A gap between scans and till discounts is a training question first and rarely a problem with the deal.
  • Takeout is separate: owners and managers see it on the Payouts page, which downloads as a CSV file.
  • Keep a one-line weekly log so you can see a pattern, not just a day.

The short answer

At close, compare how many scans your FIG dashboard shows for the day with how many deal discounts were rung in at your till. If they match, nothing more is needed. If they do not, you have found something worth a quick word with the team. The whole routine takes about five minutes.

Signing up, going live, posting deals, scanning and the dashboard cost a restaurant nothing, so this routine is only about your own time.

Why bother

A deal is a small discount you give on purpose, and you want to know what it costs and whether it is working. Two numbers tell you most of it: how many diners were scanned, and how many discounts you gave. When they agree, your staff are scanning and ringing in deals the way you trained them. When they do not, you learn it on the same day, while everyone remembers the shift.

It also feeds the bigger question of whether to keep, change or stop a deal, which reading your deal results covers.

The five-minute routine

StepWhat to doTime
1Open your FIG dashboard and note today's scans for each live deal1 minute
2Count the deal discounts rung in at the till or on order pads2 minutes
3Compare the two numbers, deal by deal1 minute
4Write one line in your log: date, scans, discounts, anything odd1 minute

If the closer is a Staff member, they can do steps 1 to 4 and leave the log for the owner or manager. Staff join by accepting an invite from the owner, with roles Owner, Manager or Staff. Setting up your team and sign-in security explains the roles.

A worked example

Say the deal is $3 off and, on a given day, the dashboard shows 12 scans. These are round example numbers, so use your own.

One day of a $3 off deal (example)
ItemNumber
Scans for the deal12
Average menu-price ticket for those tables$24.00
Menu-price sales from those tables12 × $24.00 = $288.00
Discount given12 × $3.00 = $36.00
Sales after the discount$288.00 − $36.00 = $252.00
Discount as a share of those sales$36.00 ÷ $288.00 = 12.5%

Check: $36 is one eighth of $288, which is 12.5%. If your food cost is around 30% of menu price, the food on $288.00 of sales is about $86.40, so that day's sales after the discount left about $252.00 − $86.40 = $165.60 toward wages, rent and everything else. Food cost of 28% to 32% of revenue is the range usually quoted, and 30% is the middle of it.

The point of the sum is not the exact figure. It is that you can see, in one line, what the deal cost you on that day. The slow-hour deal math turns this into a break-even question.

When the numbers do not match

Three common gaps, and what each usually means:

  • More scans than discounts rung in. A diner was scanned but the discount was never applied, so a guest may have paid full price. Check with the person at the till.
  • More discounts than scans. A discount was rung in without a scan. Remind the team that the code is scanned first and a deal code works once.
  • Scans are close but not exact. A deal with uses per diner above one will show more than one scan for one guest. If you let a deal be used more than once, up to 20 times, with an optional wait between uses, the diner's screen shows a new code after each scan.

Treat a gap as a training topic, not a blame topic. Train your staff to honour a FIG deal has a ten-minute session you can reuse.

Takeout is a separate line

Takeout orders paid in the app do not go through your dine-in till, so keep them out of the comparison. Owners and managers see a Payouts page that shows what the restaurant kept from takeout so far and this month, then lists every takeout payment, refund and fee by month, and it downloads as a CSV file. With no takeout it shows $0.00. Bank payout dates are in the restaurant's own Stripe account.

A quick check once a week is enough there. Reading your FIG Payments page goes through the columns, and takeout order math shows what an order leaves you.

A weekly log that takes a minute

One line a day makes a pattern visible after a week. A paper notebook or a shared spreadsheet both work. Keep it to the same few columns:

DateDealScansDiscounts rung inNote
Example Tue$3 off1212Busy at 2 pm
Example Wed$3 off910Check one at the till

On Sunday, add up the week and look at the days. If scans rise on a certain day, that is where a deal is working. If they sit near zero, check the hours and wording before changing the discount. Writing a deal diners understand helps with the wording.

What to do with the numbers

A daily figure is only useful if it leads somewhere. Once a week, look at the log and ask three questions. Which deal had the most scans, and on which day? Did the scans arrive in the quiet hours you meant to fill, or at your busiest time? Did the gaps between scans and discounts shrink as the week went on?

If the busy time is getting the scans, the deal may be helping people who would have come anyway, so try a narrower window. If the quiet hours are getting them, the deal is doing its job and you can leave it alone. Keep the question small: one change at a time, and one week to see what it did. The dashboard shows results deal by deal, so you can tell a deal that works from one that only looks busy.

Keep it light

A routine only works if people do it, so keep it short. Do it at the same time every night, in the same place, and let the person who closes own it. If a week goes by without a gap, you can move to checking twice a week. If you change a deal, go back to daily for a few days until you trust the new numbers.

Questions

What should I compare at the end of the day?

The number of scans per deal on your FIG dashboard and the number of deal discounts rung in at your till. They should agree.

Why would scans and discounts differ?

A scan with no discount applied, a discount applied with no scan, or a deal that lets one diner use it more than once. Each is worth a quick check.

Does FIG charge a restaurant for scanning?

No. Signing up, setting up the page, going live, posting deals, scanning and the dashboard cost a restaurant nothing.

Where do I see my takeout money?

On the Payouts page, which owners and managers can see. It lists every takeout payment, refund and fee by month and downloads as a CSV file.

Can a Staff member do the closing check?

Yes, if you want them to. The scans and dashboard check are simple enough to hand over, but the Payouts page is for owners and managers.

Can a diner use a deal more than once?

Only if the restaurant allows it. A deal can be used up to 20 times, with an optional wait between uses, and the diner's screen shows a new code after each scan.

How long should the routine take?

About five minutes. If it takes longer, trim the log to scans, discounts and one note.
