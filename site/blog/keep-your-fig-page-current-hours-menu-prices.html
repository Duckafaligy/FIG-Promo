Keep your FIG page current: hours, menu and prices

A page that is out of date costs you trust the first time a diner arrives at a closed door. A short routine for your opening hours, menu sections, photos and prices, and who on your team should look after each.

Key takeaways

  • Diners on FIG see your opening hours, menu with photos and sections, reviews and live deals, so each needs to be right.
  • Check your page on a fixed day each week. Five minutes is enough.
  • Update hours before holidays and before any closure, not after.
  • When a price changes in the kitchen or on the till, change it on your FIG menu the same day.
  • Owners and managers can look after the page. Decide who does it, so it is never nobody's job.

The short answer

Choose one day a week, open your FIG page as a diner would, and check three things: your opening hours, your menu and your prices. Fix anything that is wrong that day. Update your hours before a holiday or a closure rather than after, and give one person the job, so it is not left to chance.

Why it matters

When a diner finds your deal on the map, they are making a trip: they will walk or drive to you. If the page says you are open and the door is locked, the diner is annoyed with you, not with the page. If the menu shows a dish you stopped making, or a price that is lower than the till, the conversation at the counter starts badly.

Reviews on FIG come only from scanned visits, so every review is from a diner who really came. A visit that begins with a surprise is more likely to end with a lower rating. Keeping the page right is one of the cheapest ways to protect your stars.

Opening hours

Your opening hours are the most important line on the page. Set them to match the real days and times you serve, including any day you are closed. Then think ahead:

  • Update the hours before a holiday, not on the morning of it.
  • If you will close early for a private event or a staff day, change it as soon as you know.
  • If a deal is scheduled for a time you are going to be closed, change or end the deal too.

It helps to keep a short list of the dates you are closed each year, so you can check the page against it. The go-live checklist covers hours as part of the first setup.

Menu, sections and photos

Your menu on FIG has sections and photos. Keep the sections in the order a diner would read them, with your best-known dishes near the top. When you add or remove a dish on the printed menu, do the same online the same day.

A photo that no longer matches the plate is worse than no photo. Retake it when the dish changes. How to photograph your dishes with a phone shows how, and it takes about as long as it does to plate the dish.

Prices

Prices are where the page and the till most often drift apart. Food costs go up, a supplier changes, and the till is updated but the page is not. The result is a diner who expects $14.00 and is asked for $15.50.

A small price change, checked (example)
  • A dish on the page costs $14.00. In the kitchen it costs $4.20 to make, which is 30% of the price.
  • Your supplier's costs rise, and the dish now costs $4.90 to make.
  • At $14.00, the food cost is now $4.90 ÷ $14.00 = 35%.
  • To get back to 30%, the price would be $4.90 ÷ 0.30 = about $16.33, so you might choose $16.00 and accept a food cost of about 30.6%.

These are round example numbers. The point is to change the price on the till and on the page together, and to think about any deal that sits on top of it. A deal that was fine at $14.00 may need a second look at a new price. The slow-hour math shows how.

Who looks after the page

On FIG, staff join by accepting an invite from the owner, with the roles Owner, Manager and Staff. Decide who on the team keeps the page current. It is often a manager who knows the menu well. Put the weekly check in the same place as other recurring jobs, such as ordering or the cash-up.

Owners and managers also need a stronger sign-in for the money steps. Setting up your team and sign-in security has the details.

A five-minute weekly check

  1. Open your page as a diner would see it.
  2. Read your opening hours for the coming week, and check them against holidays and events.
  3. Scan the menu for dishes you no longer have, and for prices that have changed.
  4. Look at your live deals, and end or change any that no longer suit.
  5. Read new reviews, and reply to any that need it. Replying to reviews has a short guide.

Keep a note of what you changed and the date. If a diner says the page was wrong, you will know when it was last checked.

What to do when something is wrong

Mistakes happen. If a diner tells you the page was wrong, thank them, fix it and note the date. If you find the error yourself, fix it at once and look at the other lines for the same kind of slip. It is worth replying to a review that mentions it, in a calm, short way. You do not need to explain at length: say what was wrong and that it is fixed.

A quick tip: the next time you print a new menu, update the page first and print second. The page then never falls behind the paper.

A routine your team will keep

A routine only works if it is short. Put the five-minute check in your weekly schedule next to a task you already do. Hold it at the same time each week, so nobody has to remember. If the person who does it is away, name a backup. When the check is quick and has an owner, the page stays right without anyone thinking about it.

If you have two locations

A restaurant can have up to 2 locations on FIG for now. Check each one separately, since hours and menus often differ between them. A short list with a line per location keeps the check quick.

Questions

How often should I check my FIG page?

Once a week is a good habit, and again before any holiday or closure. Change prices the same day they change on your till.

What do diners see on my page?

Your opening hours, your menu with photos and sections, reviews and your live deals.

Who can update the page?

Your team, with the roles Owner, Manager and Staff. Staff join by accepting an invite from the owner.

What if I will be closed on a day a deal is scheduled?

Change or end the deal, and update your opening hours, so diners do not arrive to a closed door.

Where do reviews come from?

Reviews come only from scanned visits, so each one is from a diner who came to your restaurant.

Does it cost anything to keep my page up to date?

No. Setting up the page, posting deals, scanning and the dashboard cost nothing for restaurants.

How many locations can I add?

Up to 2 locations for now.
