Launching a new dish with a FIG deal: which type costs least, with the math

A new dish needs first tries. Here are four deal types on a $16 dish, what each leaves you per visit, and how to run a two-week test without guessing.

Key takeaways

  • A new dish has no reviews yet, so a deal gives people a reason to try it.
  • On a $16 dish with a 30% food cost, example deals leave between $3.20 and $10.00 a visit.
  • A side that comes with the dish costs the side's food cost, not its menu price.
  • Run the test for two weeks, change one thing at a time, and read the results deal by deal.
  • Dine-in deals carry no fee to FIG, and posting deals costs a restaurant nothing.

The short answer

Pick the deal type that costs you the least per visit while still giving a first-timer a reason to try the dish. On a $16 dish, the example below shows that a side that comes with the dish costs far less than $3 off, and a 2 for 1 costs the most per table. Run it for two weeks, read the results, then keep, change or stop.

Why use a deal on a new dish

A new dish has two problems: nobody has tried it, and there are no reviews to lean on. A deal solves the first by lowering the risk of trying it, and a scanned visit makes the second solvable, because reviews on FIG come only from scanned visits.

Start with the dish itself. Pick one that is ready, repeatable and easy for your kitchen, not something that only works on a quiet night. If it is already on your FIG menu with a photo, a deal can point to it by name. Menu photos with a phone helps with the photo.

Four deals on one dish, with the math

These are round example numbers. Say the new dish is $16 and its food cost is 30% of the menu price, so $4.80 on the plate. Food cost of 28% to 32% is the range usually quoted, and 30% is the middle of it. Dine-in only, so no fee to FIG.

One $16 dish, four deals (example)
DealGuest paysYour food costYou keep toward wages and rent
$3 off$13.00$4.80$8.20
25% off$12.00$4.80$7.20
2 for 1, one table of two$16.00 for two$9.60$6.40 a table, $3.20 a person
Free with a dish: a $4 side with the $16 dish$16.00$4.80 + $1.20 = $6.00$10.00

Check the rows: $16 − $3 = $13, and $13 − $4.80 = $8.20. 25% of $16 is $4, so $12, and $12 − $4.80 = $7.20. For the 2 for 1, two dishes cost $9.60 and one is paid for, so $16 − $9.60 = $6.40. For the included side, 30% of $4 is $1.20, so food cost is $6.00 and $16 − $6.00 = $10.00.

The included side wins on arithmetic because you give up its cost, not its price. The 2 for 1 leaves the least per person, but it brings two people to the dish. Which is best depends on what you want. Pricing a restaurant deal compares the types, and the 'free with a dish' post goes deeper on the last one.

Choosing by what you want

  • You want first tries. A dollar off or an included side lowers the risk with a small cost.
  • You want tables, not single guests. A 2 for 1 brings a second person who may not have been in before.
  • You want to protect the price. An included side keeps the dish at its menu price, so the dish never looks discounted.
  • You are worried about food cost. Use the table. If the numbers are thin on a dish, change the deal, not the recipe.

Setting it up on FIG

Everything here is in your restaurant tools, and it costs nothing to post:

  1. Add the dish to your menu, with a photo and the right section.
  2. Post the deal. Choose the type, and name the dish in the wording.
  3. Pick the hours. Live now or scheduled. A quiet hour is better than your busiest one. Live now or scheduled helps you choose.
  4. Set uses per diner. A diner can claim a deal once a day. You can let a deal be used more than once, up to 20 times, with an optional wait between uses. For a trial, one use per diner is usually enough.

A two-week test

Do not judge a new dish on one night. Give the deal two weeks, and keep a short list:

What to noteWhere to find it
Scans per day for the dealYour dashboard, deal by deal
How many of the dish you soldYour own till or order pad
What guests saidYour staff, and reviews and replies in FIG
Leftover or wasted portionsYour kitchen

After two weeks, decide: keep the deal, change one thing, or stop. Reading your deal results walks through each choice, and the slow-hour math has the break-even sum for an hour.

Reading the results

When a deal is live, the dashboard shows results deal by deal, so you can see how the new dish's deal did next to your others. Look at it every few days in the first fortnight. If scans start high and then fade, the deal may have reached your regulars and run out of new faces. If scans are low from the start, check the hours and the wording before the discount. Regulars using your deal works through that case with the numbers.

Telling people

A deal nobody knows about gets no scans. Put the dish and the deal in one line at the door and the till, and give staff one sentence to say. Telling people about your FIG deal has the wording.

If the numbers come out thin

Suppose your food cost on the new dish is 40%, not 30%. On $16, that is $6.40 on the plate. A 25% off deal leaves $12.00 − $6.40 = $5.60, and a 2 for 1 leaves $16.00 − $12.80 = $3.20 a table, or $1.60 a person. Still above zero, but thin.

When that happens, change the deal before you drop the dish:

  • Move to a smaller dollar amount, such as $2 off instead of $3.
  • Include a cheap side instead of discounting the main.
  • Run the deal in fewer hours, so it fills the quiet ones only.
  • Check the recipe. A cheaper garnish or a smaller portion of the dearest ingredient may bring food cost down without anyone noticing.

Those are examples to try one at a time, and the dashboard will tell you whether each one moved the scans.

What this costs you on FIG

Nothing beyond the discount itself. Signing up, setting up the page, going live, posting deals, scanning and the dashboard cost nothing, and there is no commission on the bill and none on dine-in. If the dish is also on takeout, FIG keeps 5% of the food after any deal and before tax on takeout orders paid in the app, and Stripe's card fee comes off the same payment. Questions

What type of deal is best for a new dish?

It depends on the goal. In the $16 example, a side that comes with the dish leaves $10.00 a visit, $3 off leaves $8.20, 25% off leaves $7.20 and a 2 for 1 leaves $6.40 a table.

How long should I run a deal on a new dish?

Two weeks is a fair test. Change one thing at a time so you know what caused a difference.

How do I work out my food cost?

Add up what the ingredients on the plate cost. A food cost of 28% to 32% of menu price is the range usually quoted for restaurants, so 30% is a good starting guess until you check.

Can I limit how often one person uses the deal?

Yes. A diner can claim a deal once a day, and you can allow more than one use, up to 20 times, with an optional wait between uses.

Do I need to pay FIG to post a deal?

No. Posting deals, scanning and the dashboard cost nothing for restaurants. There is no commission on the bill and none on dine-in.

Where do I see how the deal did?

On your dashboard, which shows results deal by deal.

Can I use a deal on a takeout order for the new dish?

Yes, if takeout is on. Takeout comes with a deal unless you set 0 deals per order, which makes takeout full price.
