Getting ready for December: holiday hours, scheduled deals and takeout on FIG

December changes your hours, your staff and your quiet days. Here is a short plan for updating opening hours, scheduling deals ahead and pausing takeout, with a worked example of a quiet-day deal.

Key takeaways

  • Update your opening hours first, so no diner arrives to a closed door.
  • Schedule deals ahead for the quiet days you can see coming, and end or pause any deal for days you will be closed.
  • Takeout has its own switch: pause it or add prep time when the kitchen is stretched.
  • In the example, a $3 off deal on a $15 dish leaves $7.50 per visit, so 8 extra visits a week is $60.00.
  • Posting, scheduling and pausing deals cost a restaurant nothing.

The short answer

Do three things before December gets busy: update your opening hours for every day that will differ, schedule the deals you want on your quiet days, and decide when you will pause takeout. All three are quick, and all three stop the same problem: a diner arriving with a claim for something you cannot serve that day.

This post is a plan you can finish in about half an hour, with one worked example so you can check a quiet-day deal against your own numbers.

Why December needs its own plan

Most restaurants do not run a normal month in December. Some days are very full, some are very quiet, and some have shorter hours or a closure. A deal that suits a normal Tuesday can be wrong on a day you are closed or booked solid.

FIG helps because you control the pieces. Deals can be live now or scheduled. Opening hours are yours to edit. Takeout can be paused. The plan below uses all three, in an order that avoids surprises.

Step 1: opening hours first

Open your page and go through every date in December where your hours will differ from usual: early closes, late openings, a closure, an event. Change the hours before you touch any deal, because a diner reads the hours on your page before anything else. Keep your FIG page current shows where to change them.

Then make a short list of the dates, with the hours for each, and tape it by the till. Staff who read the same list answer the same way when a diner asks.

Step 2: schedule the deals you want

A scheduled deal starts at the time you choose, so you can set it up in one sitting instead of remembering each morning. Pick the days and hours that are quiet for you, give each deal a clear name, and decide how many times one diner can use it. Live now or scheduled goes through the choices, and writing a deal diners understand covers the wording.

Keep the deal small and the hours tight. A diner can claim a deal once a day, and a deal can be used more than once, up to 20 times, with an optional wait between uses. For a short season, one use or a few uses is usually plenty.

A quiet-day deal, with the math

These are round example numbers, not a real menu. Say a bowl sells for $15.00, the ingredients cost 30% of that, and you post $3 off for a quiet weekday window. Dine-in, so FIG takes nothing from the bill.

One bowl with $3 off (example)
ItemAmount
Menu price$15.00
Deal− $3.00
Diner pays, before tax$12.00
Food cost, 30% of $15.00− $4.50
Left toward wages, rent and profit$7.50

Check: 30% of $15 is $4.50, and $12.00 − $4.50 = $7.50. If the deal brings 8 extra visits a week that would not have happened, that is 8 × $7.50 = $60.00 a week, or $240.00 over four weeks. If those 8 would have come at full price anyway, the deal gave away 8 × $3.00 = $24.00 a week instead.

The question is always the same: does the deal bring people who would not have come? Regulars using your deal explains how to tell.

Step 3: decide when to pause takeout

Takeout is paid in the app at checkout, and the kitchen starts when the restaurant accepts the order. On a day when the dining room is full or the kitchen is short-handed, you can pause takeout and add prep time so pickup times stay honest. Takeout comes with a deal unless you set 0 deals per order, in which case it is at full price.

Pausing takeout is separate from ending a dine-in deal, so you can do one without the other. Takeout during a rush has the details. Takeout is available only after a restaurant has finished Stripe's checks and payouts and added its HST number, so if you have not done that, dine-in deals still work without it.

Staff and the busy weeks

Busy weeks are when a deal is most likely to be misread. A new cook or a part-timer may not know that a deal is live. Before a busy stretch, spend ten minutes with the team: which deals run on which days, what the scan screen looks like, and the one sentence to say if a deal has ended. Train your staff to honour a FIG deal is a ten-minute guide you can print.

Staff can join FIG by accepting an invite from the owner, with the roles Owner, Manager and Staff. Give seasonal helpers the Staff role, so they can scan diners' codes without reaching the money steps. Owner, Manager or Staff explains who can do what.

If a day goes wrong

Even a good plan meets a bad day: a delivery that does not arrive, a sick cook, a power cut. The fastest fixes are the ones you already know. Pause the deal for the day, pause takeout, and change the hours if you must close early. Then say sorry once at the door and offer what you can do. A claim lasts as long as the deal runs, so tomorrow the same deal can work again if you turn it back on.

Write one line in a notebook about what happened, so the next busy week starts with a better plan. Small notes like that are what turn a rough December into an easier one next year.

Telling your regulars

Your page covers diners who find you on FIG. For people who walk past, a small sign by the door with the changed hours does the rest. Telling people about your FIG deal has a sign, a link and a short staff script you can reuse for the season.

What it costs

Updating hours, scheduling deals and pausing takeout cost nothing. FIG earns only on takeout orders paid in the app, keeping 5% of the food after any deal and before tax, with nothing on dine-in.

After the season

In the first week of January, open your deal results and compare each deal's scans with the quiet days you aimed it at. Keep what filled seats, and drop the rest. Reading your deal results shows how to decide.

Questions

What should a restaurant update on FIG for December?

Start with opening hours for every day that differs, then schedule or end deals to match, and decide when to pause takeout.

Can I set up deals ahead of time?

Yes. A deal can be live now or scheduled, with uses per diner and an optional wait between uses.

What happens to a claim if I end a deal for a closure?

A claim lasts as long as the deal runs. If you end the deal, anyone holding a claim can no longer use it, so end it deliberately and tell people.

Can I pause takeout without ending my deals?

Yes. A restaurant can pause takeout and add prep time. It is separate from your dine-in deals.

How many times can one diner use a deal?

You choose, up to 20 uses per diner. A diner can claim a deal once a day.

Does it cost anything to change hours or schedule deals?

No. Posting, scheduling, changing and pausing cost a restaurant nothing.

Do I need Stripe for dine-in deals?

No. Stripe's checks are needed only to turn on takeout paid in the app.
