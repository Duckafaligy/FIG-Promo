The short version

  • FIG is free: joining, your page, deals, scans and chats. No commission on the bill.
  • Takeout orders paid in the app: FIG keeps 5% of the food, and Stripe’s card fee comes off the same payment.
  • FIG Premium is optional: $50 a year, cancel anytime.
  • Honour your live deals exactly as written.
  • Only your enrolled staff devices can verify scans.

1. Who these terms are for

These terms apply to restaurants in Canada that use FIG Business. They sit alongside our Terms of service and Privacy policy. By signing up, you confirm you're 18 or older and can accept them for your business.

2. Your listing

You must run a real, licensed food business at the address you give us, and keep your details accurate. Your page goes live as soon as you finish setting it up. We can pause or remove a listing that breaks these terms.

3. Your deals

You choose your deals, times and daily limits. While a deal is live, you must honour it exactly as listed. Don't raise prices to cancel out a discount. If you pause a deal, keep honouring claims made before that. Ending a deal is what stops its claims.

4. What FIG costs

Joining FIG, setting up your page, going live, posting deals, verified scans, chats and up to 2 locations are free. There's no commission on the bill, and no sign-up, monthly, listing or per-scan fees.

Takeout orders paid in the app are paid out to you through Stripe. Setting up payouts happens inside FIG, and accepting Stripe’s terms (the Stripe Connected Account Agreement) is part of it. Your ID and bank details go straight to Stripe, never to FIG.

When a diner pays for a takeout order in the app, FIG keeps 5% of the food before tax, and Stripe’s card fee (2.9% + $0.30 for most Canadian cards) comes off the same payment. The rest, tax included, is paid out to you, and the tax is yours to remit. A takeout order the diner pays for at your counter has no FIG fee.

FIG Premium is optional: $50 CAD a year plus applicable tax, for your whole account and all its locations. It adds unlimited locations, deal announcements (one every 2 hours, to nearby diners whose cravings match your food and to diners who saved you; diners can turn them off), area insights, and a Premium badge that never changes your ranking. It renews yearly until you cancel; cancel anytime in FIG and it stays on until the end of the year you paid for. It's paid by card through Stripe, or, if you buy it through the App Store or Google Play, their payment and refund terms apply.

5. Ads

FIG doesn't sell ads right now. If we offer them later, it will be under separate terms you agree to first.

6. Wrong scans and disputes

You can report a scan you think is wrong within 14 days. We review it, and if it was a mistake we remove it.

7. Reviews

Paying FIG never changes, hides or removes reviews. You can reply to reviews in public. Don't offer anything in exchange for a review.

8. Staff and devices

You're responsible for scans made by your staff and devices. Remove access when someone leaves.

9. Diner information

You'll see a diner's first name, last initial and the deal they used, never their mobile number or email. Reach a diner only through FIG (chat, or "We're waiting" on a takeout order), and only to serve that deal or order. Don't contact diners outside FIG, and handle their information as Canadian privacy law requires.

10. Ending the agreement

You can close your business account anytime. Taps up to then are invoiced as usual. We can suspend or close your account if you break these terms, for example by not honouring deals or faking scans. If we close it for any other reason, nothing more is charged.

11. Responsibility

You're responsible for your food, prices, service and safety, and for any claims about them. FIG is provided "as is". As far as the law allows, our total liability is limited to the greater of the fees you paid us in the last 12 months or $100 CAD.

12. Changes and law

We'll give you at least 30 days' notice before changing the price. A new price applies from your next renewal. These terms follow Ontario law, and disputes go to the courts of Ontario.
