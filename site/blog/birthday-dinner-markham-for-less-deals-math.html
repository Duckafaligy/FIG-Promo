A birthday dinner in Markham for less: sorting out a group table, with the math

Celebrating with six people does not have to mean a big bill. Here is how to pick the place, use a 2 for 1 across a table, and check the arithmetic before you book.

The short answer

Pick the place by looking at live deals near you, claim before you go, and check what the deal covers for a group. A 2 for 1 on mains can take a large share off a table of six. The example below shows the arithmetic, so you can do the same with your own menu and numbers.

Start from the deals, not the restaurant

Most group dinners start with a place and end with a bill. Turn it around. Open FIG's web app at www.figpromo.ca/app, look at the map, and see which restaurants near you have a live deal. Home works as a guest, so browsing needs no account. Filter for what the group wants to eat, then read the menu and reviews.

Reviews on FIG come only from scanned visits, so they are from people who actually went. How to read a restaurant's reviews shows what to look for, and eating out with dietary needs helps when someone at the table has restrictions.

Check what the deal covers

A deal is set by the restaurant, so read its wording before you promise anything to the group. Three things to look for:

  • The type. 2 for 1, percent off, dollar off, or free with a dish.
  • The hours. A deal can be live now or scheduled, so check it runs when you want to eat.
  • The limits. Some deals can be used more than once, up to 20 times, with an optional wait between uses. A diner can claim a deal once a day.

If the wording is unclear on how many people it covers, ask the restaurant before the day. Is it a good deal? has a short checklist.

A six-person example, with the math

These are round example numbers, not any real restaurant's menu. Say the table is six people and each orders a main at $18, a drink at $4, and you share one dessert at $10.

Six people, a 2 for 1 on mains (example)
ItemWithout the dealWith a 2 for 1 on mains
6 mains at $18$108.00$54.00 (3 pairs, the second main in each pair costs nothing)
6 drinks at $4$24.00$24.00
1 shared dessert$10.00$10.00
Total before tax and tip$142.00$88.00
Per person$23.67$14.67

Check it: 6 × $18 = $108, and with three of the six not charged, 3 × $18 = $54. $54 + $24 + $10 = $88. $142 − $88 = $54 saved. $88 ÷ 6 = $14.67 a person. HST and tip come on top, and tip is easiest to work out from the menu prices.

The saving only works if the deal covers a table that size. If a deal is one per diner, each person who wants it claims their own.

Claiming, step by step

  1. Make accounts the day before. Guests can look around, but you need an account to claim, order, save or review. Diners must be 14 or older.
  2. Claim on the day. Claim in one tap on the restaurant's page.
  3. Show the code when you order. Staff scan it at the restaurant. A deal code works once, so a screenshot cannot be reused.
  4. Tell the server early. Mention the deal when you sit down, not when the bill arrives, so it is applied properly.

If a scan does not go through, what to check and what to say covers the usual causes.

Sorting out the group

The hardest part of a birthday dinner is the group itself. A few small habits help:

  • Send one note to the group early. Put the restaurant, the time and the deal in a single note in your group thread, with the link to the restaurant's page.
  • Ask about a booking for a bigger table. Call the restaurant. FIG shows places, menus, reviews and live deals, so use the phone number or hours on the page to ask about space.
  • Decide how you will split. Evenly, or by dish. Eating out as a group covers splitting the bill with a deal.
  • Leave the surprise to the cake, not the bill. Agree on the budget in advance.

A budget check before you book

Before you tell the group a price, work out a ceiling. A short sum on the back of an envelope does it:

  1. Count heads. Include everyone who will order, not only those who will claim.
  2. Add up the menu. Use the prices on the restaurant's FIG page: mains, drinks, a shared starter or dessert.
  3. Take off the deal. Use the deal's own wording, not what you hope it means.
  4. Add tax and tip. Tip is easiest to work from the menu prices, so use the same number for both versions.
  5. Divide by the number of people. That is the figure to share, with a small margin for a second round of drinks.

In the example above, $88 for six is $14.67 a person before tax and tip. Round it up to $20 in the group note and you have a cushion. Menus change, so check the page again the day before.

Where the deal does not help

Not every deal suits every table. A dollar-off deal on one dish helps little across six people ordering different things. A percent off the bill may help more. And if the restaurant is full at the time you want, a deal does not hold a table. Look at the hours the deal runs, and pick the slot that matches.

For a smaller table, date night in Markham for less and a family dinner for four use the same method.

If the deal does not fit your table

Sometimes the best-looking deal does not work for six people. Two ways round that:

  • Split into pairs for the main course. A 2 for 1 only works in twos, so seat people in pairs who order the same kind of dish.
  • Pick a different restaurant. Go back to the map and look at another place with a deal that fits a table, such as a percent off.

And if nothing fits, you can still look at the menu and reviews and decide on the food alone. A deal is a bonus, not the reason to pick a place.

After the dinner

Once the visit is scanned, you can leave a review. Reviews come only from scanned visits; deals get likes and restaurants get stars. A short, specific review helps the next group choosing a place, and writing a review on FIG shows how.
